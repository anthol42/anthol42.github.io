Lab-in-the-Loop AMP Discovery

Ongoing

Corbeil Lab, Université Laval · Summer 2025 – Present

ML lead on a closed-loop antimicrobial peptide discovery system paired with a wet-lab collaborator running a 10K-sample high-throughput potency assay. A fine-tuned transformer with a custom regression head predicts log-fold-change activity across an 870K-peptide database; top and most-uncertain predictions drive the next testing batch (~25% hit rate expected on the first round, validation ongoing). Now exploring stronger pretraining inductive bias and generative models (diffusion, GFlowNets) for direct candidate design. Sponsored by NSERC and FRQNT.

QMAP Benchmark

Published

Scientific Reports (Nature), 2026 · First author

Progress in computational AMP discovery has been hard to quantify because of inconsistent datasets and evaluation protocols. QMAP is a domain-specific, homology-aware benchmark for predicting antimicrobial potency (MIC) and hemolytic toxicity (HC50), with predefined test sets that enforce strict sequence-homology constraints between training and test data so performance reflects true generalization rather than overfitting. Reassessing existing MIC models and establishing new baselines on QMAP reveals limited real progress in the field over six years, poor performance on high-potency MIC regression, and low predictability for hemolytic activity.

Refnd

Under Review

Technical report, targeting ICLR 2027 · First author

Machine learning models trained on biochemical data are routinely evaluated using splits that fail to account for relational structure, causing information leakage and over-optimistic performance estimates. Existing splitting methods lack theoretical grounding and scale at best quadratically. We introduce the Relational Generative Process (RGP), a mathematical formalization explaining why relational structure arises in biochemical datasets, and Refnd, a splitting algorithm that leverages a proximity graph computed in loglinear time using Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW). We validate on an antimicrobial peptide dataset, showing that Refnd splits yield lower but more realistic evaluation performance than traditional splits. Refnd is applicable to any dataset arising from an RGP such as protein sequences and structures, small molecules, and nucleotide sequences, and is openly available as a Rust accelerated Python package: pip install refnd.

MALDI Deconvolution

Published

ASMS Conference, San Diego, CA, 2026 · First author (poster)

A maximum-likelihood decomposition (MLD) algorithm for identifying bacterial species in polymicrobial MALDI-TOF mixtures, where standard identification workflows break down. Reference profiles are built from Gaussian-fitted, DBSCAN-clustered peaks from pure-culture spectra, and mixtures are decomposed by scoring how well each reference profile explains the observed spectrum via a log-likelihood ratio. On synthetic mixtures the method correctly identifies all components (likelihood ratios 1.5–1.7); experimental validation is ongoing, with mass-shift calibration (lock-mass) identified as the next step to improve robustness.

HDR-Robust Visual SLAM

Ongoing

NORLAB, Université Laval · Fall 2025 – Present (part-time)

Visual SLAM robust to high-dynamic-range scenes where standard auto-exposure saturates and corrupts position/map estimates. After a deep-learning exposure predictor proved unreliable, switched to capturing multiple fixed-exposure images per frame and added custom factors so exposure changes no longer destabilize the estimate. Grew from a course project into an ongoing collaboration with NORLAB.
